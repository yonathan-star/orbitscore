ORBITSCORE / REVISION A / MUSIC DESIGN WEEK
Engineering package prepared October 25, 2026. Unbuilt design prototype.

PRODUCT
A physical-pattern percussion sequencer. A perforated disc holds one 4/4 bar,
16 sixteenth-note positions, and four instrument tracks. Infrared emitters above
and phototransistors below the disc read the holes. The performer scans the disc
by hand and then plays the captured pattern at an independent electronic tempo.

DISTINCTIVE DESIGN CHOICES
No keyboard, premade MIDI instrument kit, motor, screen, recorded drum samples,
or network account is necessary. Swapping discs changes the composition. Four
track mutes let a performer arrange the loop; two panel knobs set tempo and gain.
The four percussion voices are generated mathematically by the firmware.
This is an independently generated project concept; no claim is made that optical
sequencing itself is new. Community projects were viewed for scope, not copied.

SYSTEM
1. Six 940nm IR LEDs on a custom emitter bar, each with its own 220-ohm resistor.
2. Six BPW85C phototransistors on a custom reader bar, each with a 10k pull-up.
3. A 3.3V 74HC14 inverts and conditions all six reader signals on the controller.
4. A Pico captures four data tracks, one 16-hole clock, and one index marker.
5. Firmware commits a scan only after exactly 16 positions and the next index.
6. A sample-clock sequencer drives four synthesized percussion voices at 16kHz.
7. A MAX98357A module receives I2S and drives a 4-ohm/3W loudspeaker.

SPECIFICATION
Disc: 164mm diameter, 0.8mm thick, 6.4mm center hole.
Track radii: kick26, snare36, hat46, tom56, clock66, index76 millimetres.
Data/index holes: diameter7mm. Timing holes: diameter3mm.
Positions: 16, separated by22.5 degrees; index coincides with step0.
Scan direction: clockwise as seen from above. Reverse rotation is not detected;
rotate continuously without backing up. About2-8 seconds per revolution.
Tempo:60-180 BPM, four sixteenth-note steps per quarter note.
Pattern:16 integers, each a four-bit mask. Bit0kick, bit1snare, bit2hat, bit3tom.
Audio:16-bit mono PCM at16000 samples/sec, 128-frame blocks.
Main PCB:100x100mm; reader/emitter PCBs:90x18mm, two copper layers,1.6mm FR4.
Scan station enclosure:190x190mm. Separate controller pod:110x110mm.
Power:5V USB into Pico. No batteries or charging circuitry.

ELECTRICAL CALCULATIONS
IR branch: using1.6V nominal LED drop, (3.3-1.6)/220=7.7mA.
Six branches draw approximately46mA. Worst-case at1.1V forward drop is60mA.
One resistor at10mA dissipates22mW; standard0.25W parts give ample margin.
One detector pull-up needs at most3.3/10000=0.33mA when illuminated.
All six pull-ups add at most1.98mA. Validate optical current in the real assembly.
74HC14 and Pico GPIO use3.3V only. The audio amplifier uses5V from VBUS.
The speaker is connected across the amplifier's two output terminals; neither
terminal connects to ground. Use a regulated current-limited5V USB source rated
at least1A. Start at low volume. Validate total input current before PC-USB use.

KNOWN VALIDATION LIMITS
Desktop tests check scanner/sequence logic, sample range, file syntax, pin/net
consistency, and PCB design rules. They do not establish optical sensitivity,
ambient-light immunity, microcontroller timing, acoustic quality, regulator
headroom, or printed fit. No physical build or endurance testing occurred.
The enclosure needs a measured first fit before final fabrication quantities.
